# Salesforce market research and content direction

Research date: November 14, 2026. Method: five public-web searches through the requested Treg connector, using its TinyFish endpoint (reported total charge $0), followed by selected primary-page checks. This is directional category/positioning research, not market sizing, a hands-on competitor evaluation or measured search demand. No customer configuration was sent to Treg.

## What the evidence supports

| Category | Evidence | Implication for ContextQA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salesforce-aware test automation | Provar's own site describes metadata-aware, persona, API/SOQL and cross-application testing | Metadata-aware testing is not a unique claim; demonstrate customer-specific rule-to-evidence traceability |
| Salesforce DevOps/testing | Copado's first-party search results advertise robotic testing and API-assisted test generation | Avoid a generic “AI testing for Salesforce” differentiation claim |
| Business context and impact | Elements.cloud first-party search results describe process documentation, metadata dependencies and impact analysis | Context documentation is also an established category; show the connection to actual verification and guide maintenance |
| Enforced change | Salesforce's official OAuth retirement notice specifies February 20, 2027 | A bounded applicability and integration-verification guide has a concrete deadline; do not assume every org is affected |
| Learning | Salesforce publishes Flow testing and permission-related learning material | Complement vendor learning with a traceable customer-workflow exercise; do not recreate or imply ownership of Trailhead |

Provar's page was directly inspected. Elements.cloud and Copado observations above are first-party search-result descriptions; selected page retrieval failed, so they remain discovery-level evidence. We did not test any competing product. Do not publish comparative performance, “only platform,” pricing or feature-absence claims from this research. See the [source register](sources.md).

## Proposed position to validate

“Understand your Salesforce context, adapt reusable verification scenarios, and maintain evidence-backed guides as the org changes.”

The differentiation hypothesis is the connected workflow: approved business rule → actual org mapping → independent execution evidence → customer explanation → change-triggered review. It must be demonstrated with one customer process before claiming that it works universally.

## Audiences and the decision each needs help making

| Audience | Trigger | Useful content | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Salesforce platform owner | Upcoming seasonal change or retirement | Applicability worksheet, affected-integration inventory, proof gaps | Scope one readiness review |
| Implementation partner | UAT slipping or ambiguous acceptance | Approved rule and acceptance matrix, role/exception examples | Pilot one journey with a client owner |
| QA lead | Repetitive regression and brittle expectations | Config mapping tutorial and reusable scenario catalogue | Prepare one local custom scenario |
| Sales/service process owner | Team disagreement about actual behavior | Stated/Implemented/Observed explanation and owner review | Confirm one disputed rule |
| Enablement/documentation lead | Guides stale after changes | Evidence collection and before/after guide walkthrough | Produce one reviewed guide |

## Content priorities

Publish the helpful starter material first: onboarding worksheet, reusable-asset walkthrough, Sales/Service Cloud guides, permissions checklist, and integration readiness. Follow with an observed tenant-specific tutorial once environment bindings and actual results are verified. Keep a dated capability boundary beside every demo.

Commercial pages should invite a bounded evaluation, not promise instant full-org understanding. Pilot success metrics: time to the first reviewed scenario; share of selected rules with named owners; scenarios with complete environment/persona/result evidence; unresolved-context rate; guide updates accepted after a controlled change. Measure denominators and active human time. No ROI or price benchmark was established here.
